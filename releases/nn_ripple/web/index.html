The signal flow, live. Each source’s light shows how much of the last 50 ms it spent high; its bar is its level. The register’s cells are its bits, the newest on the left.

Faders

Logic analyser

The last two seconds of every source, sampled once a millisecond on the card. Faint lanes are turned down. Above a few hundred hertz the clock is faster than the samples, so lanes turn to noise.

Clock & cutoff

The last eight seconds, on one log frequency scale. The bands are each one’s range within every 33 ms, so noise mod and filter mod show as width.

Output & filter

Audio Out 1, about 5 ms from a rising zero crossing.
The filter’s response at the cutoff in use; the shaded band is the cutoff’s range.

Presets

    A preset is all seven pages of faders and loop lock. The clock, cutoff, resonance and filter A/B stay on the knobs and switch. Presets live in this browser; loading one sends it to the card if one is connected. The card itself keeps nothing over power-off.

    8mus

      No 8mu on this computer. Press Connect after plugging one in.

      Every 8mu on this computer has its own page, chosen with its buttons as on the card (1: Play, Logic; 2: Noise, Filter; 3: Divide, Ratios, Motion; 4: loop lock) or here. Several 8mus can play several pages at once, say one on Play and one on Logic. Two on the same page both drive it.

      Pickup is per 8mu: after a page change, a fader does nothing until it meets the stored value. A dashed line on the on-screen fader shows where a waiting 8mu fader is.

      Tilt picks which 8mu’s motion is sent to the card, for the Motion page.

      Preview

      Without a card, the page simulates the noise register, dividers, logic and feedback (not the sound) so you can design patterns. These stand in for the knobs and switch. With a card connected the real knobs are shown instead.

      Inputs & outputs

      Inputs
      Audio In 1Patched: a comparator on it replaces the noise bit, so the dividers divide any audio
      Audio In 2Clock FM, about ±2 octaves
      CV In 1, 2Clock pitch and cutoff, about 1 V/oct
      Pulse In 1Patched: rising edges step the register instead of the clock
      Pulse In 2Reseed, as switch down
      Outputs
      Audio Out 1, 2Left, right
      CV Out 1Random stepped voltage, each counter cycle
      CV Out 2The noise mod (feedback) signal
      Pulse Out 15 ms trigger each counter cycle
      Pulse Out 2The raw noise bit (/1)

      Panel

      MainClock pitch, about 0.5 Hz to 32 kHz
      XCutoff, 20 Hz to 16 kHz; with clock tune on, an interval of ±4 octaves from the clock (unison at noon)
      YResonance, or the comb’s feedback
      SwitchUp filter type A, middle type B, down (momentary) reseed
      LEDs/1 and dividers 1-5

      How it connects

      The card picks its USB mode at power-up. 8mu plugged into the card: the card reads it directly and this page isn't used. Computer plugged into the card: the card appears as a MIDI device called Ripple, and 8mus plugged into this computer are passed on by this page. After plugging a computer in, power-cycle the module.
      Help
      • Use a USB-C data cable, and close any other app using the card or the 8mus (a DAW, another editor tab).
      • Use Chrome, Edge or another Chromium browser. Safari and iOS browsers can't do WebMIDI with SysEx.
      • Press Connect card & 8mus and allow MIDI with SysEx when the browser asks.
      • On-screen faders: drag, or focus one and use the arrow keys. Double-click sets one to zero.
      • With one 8mu, clicking a page tab moves the 8mu to it. With several, the tabs only change what is shown here; move each 8mu with its buttons or its menu above.
      • With another Workshop editor open too (WaveSeq, CVSeq, Weights), the 8mus drive whichever editor you last clicked in; the header shows 8mu: here or 8mu: elsewhere.
